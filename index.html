            How to use Minifolio

            Tap a topic to open it. New here? Start with Getting started.

            Getting started
            1. A brand-new start also comes with two example recipes (Ultramarines armour and Ork skin), and the paints they use show in Your Palette. A card on the Recipes tab says so. Clear examples removes only the examples (with Undo): a paint you've used in your own recipe or given a status stays, and example paints never go on the shopping list. Keep them makes them yours. If you already had recipes, nothing is added.
            2. The app starts with three example units, marked with a These are examples card on the Dashboard and Units tab. Clear examples removes only those three (with Undo); Keep them keeps them as your own units.
            3. Add your units: one at a time with Units → + Add unit, or a whole army at once with Paste army list or Import CSV, which are on the same panel (see Importing).
            4. As you build and paint, press ▲ on a unit to move it up a stage (or tap its name and pick any stage from its Stage menu).
            5. Check the Dashboard to see how far you've got.
            6. Save a backup now and then (see Your data and backups).

            There are four tabs along the bottom: Dashboard, Units, Recipes and Settings. Help and Updates live inside Settings: open it and use the Settings / Help / Updates switch at the top. The app remembers which page you were on.

            The phone's Back button closes what's open first, one thing per press: a question box (it counts as No), a photo, the camera, a recipe, the paste box. Then it goes to the Dashboard. On the Dashboard, with nothing open, it leaves the app.

            Adding and updating units
            • + Add unit on the Units tab. The number box is how many models are in the unit (up to 999; tracking each model separately works for up to 100).
            • Add the same unit twice (same name, game and faction) and they're numbered for you: Intercessors 1, Intercessors 2.
            • Starting stage in the Add form puts a new unit straight at the stage it's really at (it stays picked for the next unit you add).
            • Each new unit is scrolled into view and outlined for a moment, so you can see where it went in the list.
            • Press ▲ to move a unit up a stage. Made a mistake? Press Undo on the message that pops up.
            • Jump to a stage: tap a unit's name and pick from its Stage menu, to go straight to any stage (forwards or back). If you track each model separately, each model has its own stage menu, and Move all models to stage moves them all at once. Every jump has an Undo.
            • When did it get there? If you track each model separately, each model says under its name when it reached its stage: Today, Yesterday, or Since 7 Oct (with the year added for an earlier year). Hold or long-press the date to see it in full. A model with no date shown has no record of when it moved.
            • Typing: while the cursor is in a text box, the tab bar at the bottom hides so it can't sit on your keyboard. Tap somewhere else and it comes back.
            • Move several units at once: on the Units tab press Select. Each unit gets a tick box (tapping a unit ticks it). Tick the ones you want, or press Select all shown to tick every unit your filters are showing. Then pick a stage from Move … to stage in the bar at the bottom. Units tracked model by model have all their models moved. One Undo puts every unit back as it was, diary included. Cancel, the phone's Back button or leaving the tab stops selecting without changing anything.
            • Tap a unit's name to open its details: its stage, photos, paint recipes and paint notes, then back a stage and delete.
            • Changing a unit: the boxes for its name, game, faction, nickname, number of models, repaint flag and Track each model separately are folded away under Edit details, near the bottom of its details. Tap it to open them. It stays open while you make changes, and shuts when you open the unit again later.
            • Questions: when the app needs to check before doing something (deleting, replacing, leaving a recipe you haven't saved) or needs a name from you, it asks in its own small box over the page. Tap Yes or No, or type the name and tap OK. No, Cancel, the Escape key and the phone's Back button all mean no, and nothing changes. On a keyboard, Enter means yes.
            • Deleting a unit: press Delete in its details and say yes. Changed your mind? Press Undo on the message that appears (it stays about 8 seconds): the unit comes back exactly as it was, photos, recipes and diary included.
            • Use the game and faction menus and the stage chips to filter the list. Tap several stage chips to combine them.
            Photos and progress timeline
            • Open a unit's details and press ＋ Add photo. You can add as many as you like, and pick several at once. Each one remembers the unit's stage and today's date.
            • The photos sit in a strip, oldest on the left, newest on the right (swipe along it; on a phone two photos fit at a time), each with a coloured dot and the stage it was at. Add one at each stage and you get a timeline of the unit going from grey plastic to painted.
            • Tap a photo to see it full size. Use ‹ › (or swipe, or the arrow keys) to move between photos, and ✕ to close.
            • In the full-size view you can fix a photo's stage or date, make it the cover (the picture on the unit's card, marked ★), or delete it. Deleted by mistake? Press Undo.
            • No cover chosen? The newest photo is used.
            • Before / after shows two photos together: the first and the latest to start with, but you can pick any two. Choose Side by side or Slider (drag to wipe from one to the other).
            • Tracking each model separately? Pick Which model? before adding a photo, so it's labelled with that model.
            Stages and "Done"
            • Stages are yours to change in Settings → Stages: rename, reorder (↑ ↓), add or remove. Changes apply when you press Save.
            • Done starts at decides what counts as finished. By default, "Battle ready", "Painted with issues" and "Parade ready" are Done.
            • Units move with their stage when you reorder. If you remove a stage, its units drop to the stage before it.
            Appearance (light and dark) and stage colours
            • Choose how the app looks in Settings → Appearance: Dark (the usual look), Light, or Match my phone, which follows your phone's own dark mode setting and switches when your phone does.
            • Settings → Colours picks the colour set for your stages: Ice, Frost, Greyscale, Colour-blind friendly, Forge (warm: ember red through amber and gold to cream), or Custom.
            • Custom lets you pick a colour for each stage. It starts from the set you had. Tap a stage's colour square to change it; the whole app updates as you go.
            • A colour that's too faint to read on the dark cards gets a ⚠ warning under its stage. You can still keep it.
            • Reset puts every stage back to the set you started from. Press Undo on the message if you change your mind.
            • Your colours stay with their stages: rename, reorder or remove stages and each keeps its colour. A new stage gets the colour your starting set gives that spot.
            • Pick a named set again at any time; your Custom colours are kept for when you switch back.
            • In light mode, pale stage colours (like the pale green "finished" colour, the white end of Greyscale, or Forge's cream) are shown a little deeper, so stage names stay easy to read on white. This works for Custom colours too. The colours you chose don't change, only how they're shown.
            • In dark mode it works the other way round: a stage colour that's a little too dark to read as text on the dark cards (some in Ice, Frost, Greyscale and Forge) is shown a bit brighter. Colours that are already easy to read are left as they are (Greyscale's run of greys is spaced out again, so its steps stay easy to tell apart). A Custom colour shown this way says so under its stage.
            • Photos and the camera always show on a dark background, like your phone's own photo viewer.
            • Your Appearance, colour set and Custom colours are saved with your other settings and in backups, so a restore brings them back.
            Faction colours
            • Give each faction its own colour in Settings → Faction colours (or tap Faction colours on the dashboard). Tap the colour circle to pick one.
            • Suggest offers a colour. If the faction's units have recipes linked, it uses those (armour recipes first, each recipe's first paint, usually the base coat). Otherwise it uses the colour the faction is usually painted, for well-known factions.
            • Use suggested colours colours every faction that hasn't got one yet, in one tap. Press Undo on the message if you change your mind.
            • The colour shows as a bar on the dashboard's faction cards, a soft tint and a dot on unit cards, and a stripe on the faction filter.
            • The coloured stripe down the left of a unit card is still its stage.
            • Rename changes a faction's name on every unit in it, and keeps its colour. Clear removes the colour.
            The dashboard
            • It counts models, not units: a 10-model squad counts as 10.
            • Tap any number, chart row or faction to jump to those units.
            • Repaint projects shows units you've flagged for a repaint.
            • Progress over time shows how many models you've added and how many are painted (Done), day by day. Touch or hover over the graph to see the numbers for any day, or open Show as a table.
            • The graph uses the dates the app has recorded since v4. Units added before that count from the start of the graph, and deleted units drop out of its history.
            Paint recipes
            • A recipe is how you paint one part, like armour, eyes or trim. Press Recipes → + New recipe.
            • Add steps in the order you paint them. Each step has a technique (base coat, wash, dry brush…), the number of coats, and one or more paints.
            • Not sure what to call a technique? Leave it on Auto and the app guesses from the paints: a shade becomes Wash, a Contrast paint becomes Contrast, 'Ardcoat becomes Varnish, and so on. You can always pick one yourself.
            • Use the Whole model part for steps done all over the model, like priming, base coats and an all-over dry brush.
            • Each step can have a short note (e.g. "heavier on the top") and an Optional tick for steps you sometimes skip. Optional steps show faded.
            • Basing materials like rocks, sand, snow and static grass can go in a step too. They're listed in Your Palette as basing materials and kept out of the colour sort.
            • For each paint, type its name and brand and tap the colour circle to pick roughly what it looks like. Paints you've used before are suggested as you type.
            • Mixing paints? Add more than one paint to a step and type a mix ratio like 2:1, 1:1:2 or 50/50 (in the same order as the paints). The %s are worked out for you, or you can set each one's mix % yourself. Contrast Medium and Lahmian Medium are suggested as you type.
            • Reorder steps with ↑ ↓. Nothing is saved until you press Save recipe.
            • Link a recipe to units from the recipe (Used on) or from a unit's details. Linked recipes show as buttons on the unit's card: tap one to open it.
            • Your Palette (top of the Recipes tab) lists every paint in your recipes, once each. It fills itself in: add a paint to a recipe and it appears.
            • In Your Palette, sort by colour, name or brand, and tap a brand to show only its paints. Tap a paint to change its colour, name or brand: the change applies in every recipe that uses it.
            • At the bottom of an open paint: Remove deletes a paint no recipe uses (a paint that's still in a recipe can't be removed). Merge into… joins it to another paint, handy for a typo twin like "Nuln Oill": its recipes move to the paint you pick, and that paint keeps its own name and brand. Both have Undo.
            • 📷 Scan a paint pot (in Your Palette) reads the paint's name from its label with the camera and suggests matches. The reading happens on your phone; the photo is never sent anywhere. The first scan loads the label reader (about 11 MB) from Minifolio's own site, and after that scanning works with no internet. Leaving the app, or closing the scan panel, switches the camera off.
            • Brands the scanner knows: Citadel, plus Vallejo Game Color, Model Color and Xpress Color, The Army Painter's Warpaints Fanatic, Pro Acryl and AK 3rd Gen (about 1,200 paints), and every paint already in Your Palette. On a Vallejo pot it also reads the code number (like 72.021), which picks the exact paint even if the name is hard to read. Other brands still work: if the scanner doesn't know the name, type it in. The same lists help when you paste recipes from your notes. Where two brands share a name (like "Black"), you're asked which one. The colours for these brands are close guesses, so tap a paint to fix its colour.
            • Typed a paint into a recipe that isn't in Your Palette yet? Above Save recipe, Do you own these? asks about the new paints: All (Owned), None (Not owned, so they go on the shopping list) or Let me pick (tap a paint to untick it).
            • 🛒 Shopping list (in Your Palette) lists paints that are running low, empty, on your wishlist, or used in a recipe but not owned, grouped by brand. Buy these at the top has a button for each shop that sells them (Firestorm Games, Wayland Games, Element Games, and The Army Painter for Army Painter paints). Tap a shop for one search link per paint; each opens that shop's own search in a new tab. They're plain links: Minifolio isn't paid for them, and only the paint's name goes to the shop. The link searches for the paint's name, so the results can include other brands' paints with the same name: check you've got the right pot before you buy.
            • Schemes (top of the Recipes tab) group the recipes you use for one faction, e.g. "My Ultramarines" = armour, trim, lenses and base. Press + New scheme, name it, type the faction and tick its recipes.
            • Apply to … units links a scheme's recipes to every unit in its faction that doesn't have them all yet. Recipes a unit already has stay linked.
            • With New units … get these recipes automatically ticked, any unit you add to that faction later (by hand or by import) gets the scheme straight away.
            • In a unit's details, the Link a recipe… menu also lists its faction's schemes. Deleting a scheme leaves its recipes and links alone.
            • Need a part or technique that isn't listed? Choose ＋ Add a new… in the menu. Remove ones you don't use under Parts and techniques.
            Pasting recipes from your notes
            • Got recipes in Google Keep, a phone note or a Reddit post? On the Recipes tab, tap Paste recipes from your notes, paste them in and press Read notes. You get a preview first: nothing is saved until you press Save.
            • Put each part on its own line ending in a colon, like Eyes:, then one step per line (or several split by commas). Mixes like 2:1 Baal Red:CM, 50/50 or Seraphim Sepia 1:2 with Contrast Medium are read as one mixed step. "Ushabti Bone and Wraithbone" is read as both paints.
            • A line with just a faction name (Ultramarines) starts that faction's scheme. A faction followed by a part (Ultramarines armour, Death Guard cloak) starts that scheme and that part, so it isn't mistaken for a paint like Ultramarines Blue.
            • Divider lines (-----, ***, ===) and lines with only emoji are skipped, like blank lines. Words the app can't read as a paint (say, in another alphabet) are kept as a note.
            • Up to 20,000 characters are read at once (a few pages of notes). If yours is longer, the preview says so: paste the rest separately.
            • Not sure about a paint (like "CM")? Check … paints asks what you meant. The app remembers your answer for next time.
            • ✕ next to a step, a part's name or a note leaves it out of the paste. Each has Undo. This only changes the preview, not your notes, so Read again brings everything back.
            • ✎ on a step lets you change it before saving: its technique, its mix ratio (Clear makes it an even mix), or Change… any of its paints to a different one. Only that step changes, and each change has Undo.
            • If the paste has paints that aren't in Your Palette yet, Do you own these? sits above Save: All (Owned), None (Not owned, so they go on the shopping list) or Let me pick.
            • Copy as text on a recipe writes it out in the same style, so you can share it or paste it back. Mixes are copied with their ratio (an even mix is written 1:1), so they come back the same.
            Importing (CSV and army lists)
            • From the Warhammer app: open a list, tap Export, copy it as text, then on the Units tab tap + Add unit → Paste army list (it's also in Settings), paste it and press Read list. Only unit names and model counts are kept: points, wargear and rules text are thrown away.
            • From a spreadsheet: save it as CSV with the columns name, game, faction, stage, nickname, count, repaint, notes, then use Import CSV (under + Add unit, or in Settings). CSV template in Settings gives you an example file.
            • The first line of the CSV must be the column titles, and one must be name. The other columns are found by their titles, so they can be in any order or left out. A file without a name column, a picture, an Excel file that isn't saved as CSV, or a file over 2 MB is refused with a message, and nothing changes.
            • Before anything changes, you choose Add to my collection or Replace my collection, and can untick any units you don't want.
            • Units you already have start unticked. If one has a different name in the import, it's highlighted so you can pick which name to keep. The Warhammer app's name is the default.
            • After an import, the Units tab opens so you can see what came in. Changed your mind? Press Undo straight after.
            Your data and backups
            • Everything is saved on this device only, in your browser. Nobody else can see it, and it isn't sent anywhere.
            • Clearing your browser's data or uninstalling the app deletes it, so Save backup regularly. The app reminds you after a week (and if you have photos, it reminds you about a backup with photos). Every backup shows a message when it's saved.
            • Save backup is small and quick, but leaves out photos. Save full backup with photos makes one bigger file with everything, photos included.
            • Send backup to… opens your phone's share menu, so you can put the small backup straight into OneDrive, an email or a message. It's the same file as Save backup (no photos). If your phone can't send files from the app, or sending fails, the backup is saved to your device instead and the app says so. Closing the share menu changes nothing.
            • Restore backup brings either kind back, including your stages, recipes, schemes, faction colours, stage colour set and Appearance (light or dark). A backup without photos keeps any photos already on this device that match.
            • Photos are kept in a separate, much bigger store in your browser, so there's room for lots of them. Settings → Your data shows how many photos you have and how much space they use.
            • Clean up photo storage removes photo files no unit uses any more (for example after Delete all units) and tells you how much space it freed. Photos in the device's safety copy are left alone.
            • Export CSV saves your collection as a spreadsheet (without photos).
            • If the device's storage is full, a red banner appears at the top saying changes aren't being saved. It stays until saving works again. Save a backup, then free some space. Big changes (new stages, a restore) are only made if they can be saved, so nothing gets half-saved.
            • Delete all units, Replace my collection and Restore backup keep a copy of what you had on this device for 30 days. Find them under Settings → Your data → Recover earlier data. Delete all also has an Undo button. If there isn't room for the copy, Delete all and Replace stop and change nothing.
            • If some photos in a backup can't be loaded, the app tells you how many. Keep the backup file safe and try again later.
            • A backup with recipes but no units can be restored too: it replaces only your recipes. It has an Undo button, and your old recipes are also kept for 30 days under Recover earlier data as "Before Restore backup (recipes only)".
            • Your data and backups carry a data version number. If an older copy of the app (say an installed app that hasn't updated yet) finds data saved by a newer one, it stops saving and asks you to reload, so it can't damage that newer data. A backup saved by a newer version asks you to update before it restores.
            • A Saved message only shows when your change really was saved. If the device's storage is full you'll see Not saved with a Save backup button instead. A paste import that can't be saved is put back and stays open, so your answers aren't lost.
            • Got the app open in two places at once (say the installed app and a browser tab)? Changes made in one now show up in the other, instead of being saved over.
            • Restore backup checks the file first. A picture or other file picked by mistake, a file over 300 MB, or a backup that's damaged is refused with a message, and nothing changes.
            • New phone? Save a backup on the old one, open the app on the new one, and restore it.
            Installing on your phone
            • Open the app's web address in Chrome, then use the menu (⋮) and choose Add to Home screen or Install app.
            • It works with no internet. Updates arrive next time you open it online; you may need to close and reopen it once.
            • On an iPhone, use Safari's Share button → Add to Home Screen. This matters: iPhones can clear a website's saved data (photos included) if it isn't opened for a week, but not for apps added to the home screen.
            Updates and feedback
            • The version number is next to the title. The Updates tab lists what changed in each version.
            • Found a bug or have an idea? Use Give feedback under Settings → Updates.
            Privacy
            • Stays on your phone: your units, photos, recipes, paints and settings. They're saved in this browser on this device only. There's no account and no cloud copy.
            • Scanning a paint pot reads the label on your phone. The photo never leaves it.
            • What goes out: only what you choose to send. Give feedback opens a form on Tally's website, which receives what you type there plus the app's version number. Backups and exports are files you save yourself. Send backup to… only opens your phone's own share menu: the backup goes where you choose, and the app sends nothing itself.
            • Shop links (Buy these, on the shopping list) open the shop's website only when you tap one. The link carries just the paint's name for the shop's search, and the shop isn't told it came from Minifolio. From then on you're on the shop's site, under its own privacy rules.
            • Locked down: the app only runs its own code, from its own files. Your browser is told to refuse scripts from any other website, or slipped into the page itself, and the app itself can't quietly send your data to another website (a “Content Security Policy”).
            • No tracking: no analytics, no adverts, no cookies, and nothing is sold.
            • Like any website, the site's host (GitHub Pages) sees basic technical details, such as your internet address, when the app loads or updates. Minifolio itself never sees or keeps them.

Other paint names (such as Vallejo, The Army Painter and AK Interactive) belong to their makers. All are used only to identify those products.
